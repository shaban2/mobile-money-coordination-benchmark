## Interpretation for this repository — assessment, not literature findings

An exploratory benchmark paper is a reasonable direction to develop; acceptance and novelty are not established by this search. The candidate contribution is the auditable comparison under a common asynchronous client contract, explicit durable-completion accounting, ledger invariants, and descriptive fault-response evidence. Do not claim to be the first REST/Kafka or orchestration/choreography comparison.

Suggested working title: **An Exploratory Evaluation of REST and Kafka Coordination for Synthetic Mobile-Money Transfers**.

The current implementation is a pair of single-process coordination applications with co-located workflow handlers, synthetic provider mappings, a common PostgreSQL ledger, and an external broker for Kafka. It is not an independently deployed domain-microservice system or a real telecom settlement network. Repository basis: [manuscript alignment](../docs/MANUSCRIPT_ALIGNMENT.md) and [approved six-run sequence](../docs/EXPLORATORY_FOLLOWUP_V1.md).

Reporting boundaries for the existing data:

- Treat the six new runs as three exploratory matched pairs: 8 operations/s no-fault screening (2m warm-up/4m measurement), and two fault scenarios at 4 operations/s (5m/10m each). Report their unequal rates/durations; do not pool them or interpret cross-scenario latency differences as isolated causal fault effects.
- Retain earlier calibration and unsuccessful/interrupted attempts with their provenance and dispositions. Different revisions must not silently become independent repeats of the final implementation.
- State one pair per new scenario. Show observed outcomes, distributions and time trajectories, not population-level superiority, practical equivalence or confidence intervals manufactured by treating requests/windows as independent runs.
- Keep capacity, percentage-of-capacity claims and the provisional 96-run confirmatory plan out of the claims for this narrower report. Preserve them as unexecuted plans/future work, rather than retroactively changing the frozen evidence.
- Separate admission/202 latency from application-observed terminal COMMIT acknowledgement; separate correct goodput from all workload operations. Both architectures use an asynchronous public contract.
- Report adapter stop/restart as the fault actually performed, not exhaustive abrupt-crash safety. A lack of observed ledger violations is not proof of universal exactly-once behavior.
- Recovery and backlog clearance are window-based, descriptive endpoints. Similar recorded recovery times do not demonstrate statistical equivalence.
- Explain that the exploratory scope was selected after pilot observations. Do not claim prospective preregistration of that selection.

Remaining paper work is focused: draft the narrower questions and contribution, generate figures/tables from preserved artifacts, explain the run history and limitations, verify the closest full-text comparisons, and assess venue fit. A bounded extra replication set might strengthen the paper, but no numerical minimum or new execution is adopted here.

- Adapter unavailability at 4 operations/s: 5-minute warm-up and 10-minute measurement per architecture.
- Database-response delay at 4 operations/s: 5-minute warm-up and 10-minute measurement per architecture.

All six summaries pass the recorded qualification gates. Matched workloads, durable-completion accounting, ledger checks, fault evidence and retained artifacts are useful strengths. Earlier calibration and screening results remain useful with their own provenance; they are not automatically fresh repeats of these three conditions.

**Enough to write a narrowly descriptive exploratory benchmark report: yes. Enough to establish a repeatable general performance advantage, capacity boundary or submission acceptance: no.** This is an assessment of claim support, not a prediction of a venue's decision. Novelty and presentation still require evaluation.

The principal comparative-evidence weakness is one independent pair per scenario, not an insufficient number of requests within each run. Thousands of requests show within-run behavior but do not establish how outcomes vary across fresh executions. Conditions also have unequal rates and durations; compare architectures within matched scenarios, not the 8/s no-fault run against the 4/s fault runs as an isolated causal fault effect. Within-run pre-fault/recovery trajectories remain descriptive evidence.

Recommendation: prepare the narrower paper and figures now. If strengthening comparative repeatability, prioritize a separately approved, fixed-budget replication of existing conditions with unchanged settings and balanced architecture order. Specify the budget, endpoints, validity rules and reporting/stopping policy before those new runs; do not stop merely when a preferred conclusion appears. A same-load no-fault control would be needed for a stronger fault-versus-no-fault comparison. No numerical repetition minimum, capacity campaign, source change, or new run is adopted by this note.

### What this repository actually generates

This is a custom, deterministic, synthetic P2P transfer API workload, not a replay of production customer traces and not an implementation of SmallBank or another established benchmark suite. k6's constant-arrival-rate executor schedules workload iterations at a fixed offered operations/second rate. Each iteration issues one public API request; internal workflow calls, retries, callbacks and broker events are additional system work, not extra offered client operations.

For every complete 100-iteration cycle there are 76 unique-transfer creation attempts, four deliberate replay submissions and 20 GET status requests. Equivalently, creation submissions comprise 80% of operations, with 5% of those submissions being replays. Replays reuse the immediately preceding iteration's payload and idempotency key. Status reads target a single separately created setup transfer, not the individual measured transfers. Provider-profile submissions are balanced A/B over a complete cycle.

Payloads are calculated from iteration indexes: numbered payer/payee identifiers, amount `100 + (sourceIteration % 900)`, currency `UGX`, a run-specific client reference, and a provider profile. The code's amount convention is integer minor units. `RANDOM_SEED` is hashed to an account-index offset; it does not load a seed-data file or randomly select every payload field. These are designed operation proportions, not measured telecom traffic proportions. Partial cycles can have small composition differences.

At 4 operations/s for a ten-minute measurement, the scheduled 2,400 client operations comprise 1,824 unique-transfer attempts, 96 replay submissions and 480 status reads, excluding setup and warm-up. Therefore, an observed correct goodput of 3.04 unique transfers/s can account for all unique-transfer attempts at this offered rate. The latest no-fault screen uses 8 operations/s for four measured minutes; the two fault scenarios use 4 operations/s for ten measured minutes. Fault schedules are adapter unavailability for 30 seconds or 100 ms database-response delay for 60 seconds, beginning at measurement second 240.
